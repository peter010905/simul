# Mixed v1.5.1 experimental short relook for tiny decoded patterns

R3 remains independently runnable. This revision adds one optional use for a native-size OpenCV Original-dictionary decode with a shortest edge of 8 to under 10 pixels. Such a decode previously fell below the ten-pixel admission threshold even if the local print pattern matched the decoded bits.

## When a look can be requested

The four native image corners must be inside the normal image margin and pass the existing printed-pattern check. The detector examines up to five native quads in this size band per exposure. A busier frame, a box containing repeated ambiguous IDs, or an exposure with a stronger normal decode cannot issue a conflicting tiny request. The two-times upscaled pass continues to serve normal marker processing.

The mission requires two independent exposures of the same ID, spaced 0.15–0.85 s apart. They must agree in calibrated odometry bearing within 2.2°, camera origin within 0.22 m, apparent size ratio within 0.80–1.25 and normalized image height within 0.10. Duplicate image stamps, alternating IDs on the same bearing and multiple plausible simultaneous matches cannot corroborate an ID. The detector uses the exposure TF and undistorted camera ray. The mission checks the latest TF again at receipt and accepts a 6° turn only when it still reduces the optical-axis error. Existing named-marker work, recoveries, near-pass inspections, hazards and the final eight seconds take precedence.

The new cue and the existing outgoing-edge cue share two short looks per mission and a 30 s cooldown. A requested near-pass look on the same bearing and from the same neighborhood is deferred for 15 s after the short look. An active near-pass inspection and its existing 15 s cooldown also take precedence. A tiny cue supplies no depth, owns no destination, and cannot populate the registered target map. The message carries separate fields for tiny decoded prints and anonymous bearings; only the latter can enter wall memory. Normal ID registration, Nav2 and clearance checks retain their existing inputs.

## Reproduction

Apply `integration.patch` to the four Python files in the frozen R3 package with `patch -F 0 -p1`, then place `tiny_named_relook.py` in the same package. The staged installer enforces this lineage. Run `python -m unittest discover -s <candidate-directory> -p test_tiny_named_relook.py` with the package on `PYTHONPATH`. The detector-to-mission test needs Jazzy ROS message types. Other tests exercise the installed package and OpenCV Original dictionary directly.

Run [the passive evaluator](../../experiments/2026-11-22_v151_adaptive_survey/development/tiny_named_replay.py) on the 112605 development excerpt and the three full archived rescue360 runs on maps 112602–112604. [Per-run measurements](../../experiments/2026-11-22_v151_adaptive_survey/development/tiny_named_results.json) include both the inputs eligible for the branch and the actual requests generated by the image-and-bearing logic.

## Evidence and limits

On development map 112605, OpenCV decoded the box-mounted 32 on seven saved 480×480 images between 189.852 s and 199.252 s. Its shortest image edge ranged from 6.00 to 9.06 px. Four frames passed the existing printed pattern check, two at 8 px or greater. Those two captures were 0.600 s and 0.154 m apart, with a 1.02° change in world bearing. The earlier policy admitted zero normal ID observations in these frames. The new branch requested a left 6° glance at 194.852 s when the printed center was 28.59° to the left of the camera under the 80 ms receipt simulation. Even perfect execution of this turn would leave 22.59° off axis. The original route selected an interior-surface goal at 184.86 s and an interior-frontier goal at 207.86 s.

All seven source pictures are lossy JPEGs captured at the RGB header stamp. The original detector subscribed to raw mono8. Its exposure queue dispatched 3,011 of 3,014 received frames. The three drops cannot account for seven discarded JPEG decodes, but per-frame raw images, ROS TF histories and Nav2 availability at receipt were not archived. Offline corroboration yields an opportunity to request a glance. A successful spin, reacquisition, registration and approach all require a new closed-loop run. The mandatory 6° segment alone would take at least 0.21 s at an angular speed of 0.5 rad/s, with further time possible for cancellation, acceleration, settling and replanning.
